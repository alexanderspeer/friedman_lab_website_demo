Our mission

Understanding the brain by listening where it matters most.

The Friedman Lab investigates how distributed brain networks give rise to movement, cognition, and behavior. By pairing intracranial recordings with precisely designed tasks, we seek discoveries that can directly inform care.

Current research projects

Four active lines of inquiry spanning network neuroscience, computational modeling, clinical imaging, and neurosurgical device development.

01
Network NeuroscienceParkinson's Disease

SCAN–STN connectivity & deep brain stimulation

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) of the subthalamic nucleus (STN) improves motor symptoms in Parkinson's disease but can also impair cognitive control. We investigate whether individual differences in connectivity between the somato-cognitive action network (SCAN) and STN are associated with these motor and cognitive effects. By combining precision functional neuroimaging, visuomotor behavioral assessments, and DBS ON/OFF testing, we aim to characterize the contribution of cortical-subthalamic networks to stimulation outcomes and inform future approaches to personalized neuromodulation.

02
Computational NeurosciencePredictive Processing

Free Energy Principle & sensorimotor inference

We investigate how the brain infers changes in the relationship between actions and their sensory consequences. Motivated by the Free Energy Principle and predictive processing frameworks, we use a visuomotor task in which participants encounter unexpected changes in joystick-to-cursor mappings and varying levels of visual feedback reliability. By manipulating prior experience, environmental uncertainty, and sensory information, we examine how behavior adapts to changes in hidden sensorimotor states. These experiments provide a framework for studying belief updating, sensorimotor adaptation, and the neural mechanisms of inference.

03
Focused UltrasoundQuantitative Motor Analysis

Computer vision & machine learning for motor assessment

We investigate computational methods for quantifying motor behavior in patients undergoing focused ultrasound (FUS) treatment for movement disorders. Using computer vision and machine learning, this research explores the extraction of objective movement measurements from video recordings, including tremor characteristics and changes in motor performance. The goal is to develop reproducible methods for characterizing movement abnormalities and evaluating motor responses to therapeutic interventions.

04
Neurosurgical Device EngineeringHydrocephalus

Endoscopic third ventriculostomy stent development

Endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV) treats obstructive hydrocephalus by creating an opening that allows cerebrospinal fluid to bypass an obstruction. However, closure or narrowing of this opening can result in treatment failure. This project investigates the development of an implantable stent intended to maintain long-term patency of the ventriculostomy. The research combines neurosurgical expertise with biomedical device engineering to explore approaches for reducing recurrent obstruction and the need for additional surgical intervention.

Collaboration

Brunner Lab

Washington University in St. Louis
Peter Brunner, PhD

The Friedman Lab collaborates with the Brunner Lab at Washington University in St. Louis on human neurophysiology and neuromodulation research. Our collaborative work integrates intracranial electrophysiology, behavioral experimentation, and synchronized neural and kinematic data acquisition to investigate the neural mechanisms of movement and cognitive control.

Through shared experimental infrastructure and the BCI2000 platform, we develop and implement behavioral paradigms for studying cortical and subcortical brain activity in clinical research settings.

Open science

Research software & documentation

Tools we develop and maintain to make behavioral neuroscience experiments more reproducible and accessible.

01

Visuomotor joystick task

BCI2000 ApplicationMotor control & inhibitory control

A BCI2000 visuomotor reaching task designed to investigate automatic and controlled movement. Participants operate a joystick under normal and inverted cursor mappings, allowing measurement of reaction time, movement trajectories, and motor performance across experimental conditions.

View documentation
02

FEP visuomotor task

BCI2000 ApplicationPredictive processing & sensorimotor inference

A behavioral paradigm motivated by the Free Energy Principle, designed to investigate how individuals adapt to unexpected changes in sensorimotor mappings. The task manipulates prior experience, hidden mapping transitions, and visual sensory reliability to study behavioral adaptation and inference under uncertainty.

View documentation
03

BCI2000 to BIDS converter

Python · Open sourceNeurophysiology data processing

An open-source software pipeline for converting BCI2000 recordings into datasets organized according to the Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS). Supports electrophysiological data, behavioral events, motion recordings, and associated metadata, with configurable conversion profiles and tools for reproducible data organization.

Research opportunities

The Friedman Lab at Washington University in St. Louis welcomes inquiries from students and researchers interested in human neurophysiology, neuromodulation, and computational neuroscience.

Our research integrates intracranial electrophysiology, precision neuroimaging, behavioral experimentation, and computational analysis to investigate the neural circuits underlying movement and cognition. We are interested in working with individuals from diverse academic and technical backgrounds, including neuroscience, biomedical engineering, computer science, cognitive science, and related disciplines.
